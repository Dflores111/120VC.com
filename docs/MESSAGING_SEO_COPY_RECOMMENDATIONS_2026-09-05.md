# 120VC Messaging + SEO Copy Recommendations

Date: September 5, 2026  
Status: Applied September 6, 2026 — responsive copy and QA refinements included

## Executive decision

The architecture is strong. The primary copy problem is not a lack of good options; it is that too many phrases are trying to define the company at once.

The site should consistently do three things in this order:

1. Name the problem or outcome in language an enterprise buyer already uses.
2. Explain, plainly, what 120VC changes in the work.
3. Introduce “Execution Leadership” and the “Execution Leadership System” only after the practical value is clear.

Search terms should identify the subject of a page. They should not dictate the voice of the page.

## The messaging spine to lock

### Primary promise

**Turn strategy into results your teams can deliver.**

Why this wins: it is clearer and more concrete than “Make high-stakes work more predictable.” It says what moves, what the outcome is, and who delivers it without requiring the visitor to understand 120VC terminology.

### Plain-English explanation

120VC works inside your highest-priority work to help leadership teams set clear priorities, make decisions, assign ownership, and deliver—without constant executive rescue.

### Distinct mechanism

120VC installs a shared execution system inside work already underway.

### Category

120VC calls this discipline **Execution Leadership**.

### Desired state

**Clear priorities. Decisions that hold. Teams that deliver.**

### Primary next step

**Book a Consultation**

Use “Request a Conversation” when the action is a form submission. Avoid “Book a Leadership System Consultation” until the visitor has learned what the system is.

## Copy rules

- Headline first, useful description second, component third. Do not add an eyebrow merely because the design has space for one.
- A page reached from “What We Solve” should immediately say what 120VC solves. A page reached from “How We Help” should immediately say how 120VC helps.
- Keep major headlines to one or two lines at common desktop widths; three is the absolute exception.
- Use the full list—priorities, decisions, ownership, capacity, operating rhythm, accountability, outcomes—only once on a page. Use shorter references elsewhere.
- Use “install” at key moments where it differentiates 120VC. Repeating it in every section makes a distinctive idea feel mechanical.
- Use “actually” sparingly. It currently appears often enough to become a verbal tic.
- Reserve small labels for real orientation: Case Study, carousel position, step names, tool type, or an input instruction.
- Do not repeat a headline in a label, description, CTA, or adjacent section.

## Primary-navigation page decisions

### Home

Recommended section sequence and visible copy:

1. **Turn strategy into results your teams can deliver.**
2. Logo marquee with no decorative headline. Use an accessible label in the component.
3. **Five execution problems we solve.**
4. **Clear priorities. Decisions that hold. Teams that deliver.**
5. **We help leadership teams fix execution across three core areas.**
6. **See what changed.**
7. **We install the system in four stages.**
8. **Bring us the problem that keeps returning.**

Supporting copy:

- Problem recognition: “The strategy can be sound. Work still breaks down when priorities compete, decisions reopen, ownership is unclear, and teams are asked to absorb too much.”
- Service paths: “Start with the leadership team, the transformation, or the portfolio and critical work.”
- Proof: “Each case study shows the condition before 120VC, what changed in the work, and the result.”
- Final CTA: “You do not need to know which service fits. Start with what needs to move and what keeps getting in the way.”

What not to use:

- “Make high-stakes work more predictable.” It is elegant but too abstract for the primary promise.
- “Where execution problems actually begin.” It sounds like an article title and does not tell the visitor that 120VC solves these problems.
- “Book a Leadership System Consultation.” It introduces the proprietary mechanism too early and creates a longer, less human CTA.

### What We Solve

**H1:** We solve problems that stall critical work.

**Alternative 1:** We solve the problems that keep strategy from becoming results.  
**Alternative 2:** When critical work keeps stalling, the execution problem is usually underneath it.

Why the recommendation wins: it answers the navigation promise immediately, names the category naturally, and leaves room for the five cards to provide recognition.

Recommended section headlines:

- **Which problem keeps coming back?**
- Dynamic selected detail: **Here is what is driving the problem.**
- **Choose the kind of help you need next.**
- **Different symptoms. The same broken execution chain.**

Use these five buyer-facing problem names:

1. Priorities keep changing.
2. Decisions keep coming back.
3. Transformation is not changing the work.
4. Too many projects are competing for the same people.
5. An internal function is busy but cannot prove its value.

Keep the more formal terms—strategy drift, leadership fragmentation, transformation stall, portfolio overload, reactive internal function—for metadata, internal taxonomy, or supporting copy. They should not be the first language a buyer must decode.

### How We Help

**H1:** We help leaders fix how work gets done.

**Alternative 1:** We improve execution where the work keeps breaking.  
**Alternative 2:** We help leaders change the way important work is prioritized, owned, and delivered.

Why the recommendation wins: “Choose where execution needs to change” asks the buyer to diagnose the solution. The recommended line states 120VC’s role first.

Recommended section headlines:

- Service group: no second headline if the three cards immediately follow the hero.
- Leadership: **Help the senior team decide and deliver as one.**
- Transformation: **Make transformation part of normal work.**
- Portfolio and projects: **Put people and capital behind the work with the strongest value.**
- Shared method: **Every service changes the same five things.**
- Proof: **See what that change looks like in the work.**

Supporting copy for the shared method:

“Priorities become explicit. Decisions hold. Ownership is visible. Risks surface earlier. Results stay connected to the work.”

### How It Works

**H1:** We install a shared execution system inside live work.

**Alternative 1:** See how 120VC changes the way critical work runs.  
**Alternative 2:** One system connects priorities, decisions, ownership, and results.

Why the recommendation wins: it tells the visitor what 120VC does and where it happens. “Diagnose the break. Install the system. Build ownership.” is a sequence of taglines, not an explanation.

System component headlines:

| Part | Headline | What becomes visible |
|---|---|---|
| Intent | Define what must change—and why. | The business condition, outcome, and reason the work matters. |
| Priorities | Decide what deserves capacity. | The work that moves now, later, or not at all. |
| Decisions | Make a decision that will hold. | The problem, viable options, recommendation, authority, and rationale. |
| Commitments | Make ownership visible. | The owner, outcome, date, dependencies, and next commitment. |
| Execution | Run the work under real pressure. | Progress, blockers, risk, changes, and decisions that need attention. |
| Outcomes | Measure whether the work created value. | The customer, team, and business results the work was meant to produce. |

Installation section:

- **We install it in four stages.**
- Diagnose: “Find where priorities, decisions, ownership, or operating rhythm are breaking.”
- Install: “Put the new rules, practices, roles, and artifacts into the live work.”
- Operate: “Use the system in weekly work, real decisions, and real business pressure.”
- Own: “Transfer the capability so the team can keep running it without 120VC.”

First-value section:

**The first changes leaders can see.**

- A real priority is clarified or deprioritized.
- A decision closes with one owner and a visible commitment.
- A blocker surfaces early enough to change the outcome.

### Results

**H1:** See what changed—and the result it produced.

**Alternative 1:** See the results of changing how teams execute.  
**Alternative 2:** From execution problem to measurable result.

Recommended section headlines:

- **Before. What changed. After.**
- **Find the case closest to your situation.**
- **See what sits behind the number.**
- **Need evidence your buying group can inspect?**

Publishing rule: do not keep a public Results page in the primary navigation as a promise if it only says case studies are being prepared. Either publish at least one approved case with the full evidence record or temporarily route proof through approved logos and method evidence elsewhere.

### Resources

**H1:** Tools for clearer priorities and decisions.

**Alternative 1:** Tools for the execution problems leaders face every week.  
**Alternative 2:** Explore ways to make the priority, decision, or commitment in front of you clearer.

Implementation guard: use “Explore” until the public tools are approved and actually interactive. “Use” and “Build” promise an active workspace that the current production-safe routes do not yet provide.

Recommended section headlines:

- **Use a tool on the work in front of you.**
- **Understand why the problem keeps returning.**
- **Start with _The Strategy Execution Gap_.**
- **When understanding is not enough, change the work.**

## Keyword decisions

### Strong page owners

- Homepage: strategy execution; strategy implementation; strategy to execution.
- What We Solve: strategy execution problems; execution gap; competing priorities; decision bottlenecks.
- How It Works: strategy execution framework; leadership operating system; decision rights; operating rhythm.
- Leadership Team Transformation: leadership team development. Use coaching terms only to clarify the difference.
- ELPA: executive leadership development program. Do not center broad executive coaching language.
- Enterprise Transformation: business transformation consulting; transformation consulting; value realization.
- Portfolio Services: project portfolio management; enterprise project portfolio management; PMO consulting.
- Project Leadership: enterprise-qualified project management consulting and program management.
- Smartsheet: Smartsheet project portfolio management; portfolio visibility.
- Tools: prioritization matrix, project prioritization matrix, decision-making framework, leadership meeting agenda template.

### High-volume terms that require guardrails

- **Digital transformation consulting:** strong volume, but heavily biased toward technology implementation. Do not make it the Enterprise Transformation page’s primary promise.
- **Executive coaching services:** strong volume, but likely to attract buyers seeking one-to-one coaching. Use only as an intercept and quickly explain the live-work/team difference.
- **Project management consulting:** useful demand, but broad and partly construction, IT, or staffing oriented. Qualify with “enterprise,” “critical initiatives,” and “embedded leadership.”
- **Project portfolio management tools:** software-heavy intent. Keep it subordinate to the Smartsheet page or educational content.
- **Operating model:** high volume, but too broad for the homepage. Use only where the page genuinely covers how work operates.

## SEO evidence boundary

The supplied volume data is useful for comparing market language, not for proving which queries produce qualified 120VC buyers. The research notes show that the connected Keyword.com project has no tracked keywords and Google Search Console is not connected. Before using volume to make major navigation or page-ownership decisions, connect first-party search data and evaluate which queries and landing pages produce qualified accounts and consultation requests.

## Redundancy audit

The following ideas are currently repeated too often across the brief and site:

- “inside live work”
- “one shared way”
- “priorities, decisions, ownership, operating rhythm, and outcomes”
- “what keeps breaking”
- “actually”
- “install”
- “execution” in adjacent headings

These are important ideas, but repetition does not automatically create clarity. Give each phrase a home:

- Homepage owns the promise.
- What We Solve owns the recurring problems.
- How We Help owns the service choices.
- How It Works owns the system and installation language.
- Results owns evidence.
- About owns the formal category name and philosophy.
