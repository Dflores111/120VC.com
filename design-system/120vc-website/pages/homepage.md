# Homepage Conversion Reset

## Page job

Help an enterprise leader understand the result 120VC creates, recognize credible experience at enterprise scale, remember the core operating value, recognize that 120VC understands the condition, see the available commercial scopes, understand why the delivery model is different, and take one useful next step.

The homepage must answer without interaction:

1. What does 120VC help create?
2. Is this relevant to the condition I am carrying?
3. What can I engage 120VC to do?
4. How is this different from training, coaching, software, or advice-only consulting?
5. Why should I believe it?
6. What should I do next?

Primary next step: **Book a Leadership System Consultation.**

Secondary learning path: **See how 120VC helps.**

## Component grammar

This page-specific grammar instantiates the global component-semantics rule in `../MASTER.md`. Use components according to the mental model a normal website visitor already brings:

| Content | Component | Implied meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One value promise | Stable hero copy | This is the main point |
| Human pressure and coordinated action | Cinematic hero media | This is the emotional and operating context |
| Approved enterprise experience | Pausable logo marquee | Scan parallel brand marks as a restrained authority cue, not outcome proof |
| Brand thesis | Pausable text marquee | Remember the three operating outcomes through one continuous sentence band |
| Parallel pain points | Manual card carousel | Browse peer conditions |
| More about one pain point | Explicit flip disclosure | Reveal the consequence and relevant solution |
| Parallel commercial scopes | Editorial cards with optional disclosure and explicit links | Compare ways 120VC can help, then reveal secondary detail or navigate |
| Ordered installation | Manual four-step stepper | Explore stages that happen in sequence |
| Approved cases | Story or quote carousel | These are independent evidence examples |
| Final conversion | Still CTA section | Make the next decision without distraction |

The complete Problem Explorer belongs on What We Solve. The complete System Explorer and six-node Execution Spine belong on How It Works.

## Current audience journey with two transition bands

1. **Position / deep ink**

   Stable outcome-led H1, one direct supporting explanation, one supplied human image with finite cinematic drift, a consultation CTA, and a quiet route to How We Help. Abstract micro-taglines do not sit beneath the core message. The image has a visible pause control and becomes a still under reduced motion.

2. **Trust transition / white**

   The exact 17-brand pack supplied and approved on 2026-08-27 appears in a calm, non-clickable logo rail beneath the hero. The statement is limited to “120VC has worked with leaders and teams at.” A visible pause/play control, focus/hover pause, tab-hidden pause, and static reduced-motion grid are required.

3. **Recognition / clay**

   Five manual, image-led pain cards in a scroll-snap carousel. Show about three cards on desktop and one-plus on mobile. Previous and next controls remain visible. No autoplay. Each explicit reveal shows only cost, change, and the relevant solution. Essential recognition remains on the front.

4. **Memory transition / paper**

   After the visitor recognizes the problem, one kinetic sentence repeats: “CLEAR PRIORITIES • DECISIONS THAT HOLD • TEAMS THAT DELIVER.” Phrases and orange bullets are separate elements. It moves in the same direction as the logo rail at a faster rate, has a visible pause/play control, and becomes one complete wrapped statement under reduced motion.

5. **Commercial solution / white**

   Three editorial cards: Leadership Performance, Enterprise Transformation, and Portfolio & Project Execution. The offer, buyer value, and explicit destination remain visible without interaction. A real button reveals the secondary “What changes first” detail; a separate link navigates. The static card surface does not impersonate a control. The chapter ends with the cards; no consultation-routing sentence or filler transition sits between the service scope and proof.

6. **Client results / paper and deep ink**

   After the visitor understands what 120VC can be hired to do, one editorial case-study carousel answers “Has this actually worked?” One story dominates. The transformation title leads and enterprise context stays quiet. A familiar Before → 120VC Changed → After comparison is the only explanatory system; the one primary result belongs inside After. One sentence states what 120VC installed, and one attributed quote closes the narrative. Supporting metrics and abstract diagrams do not render on the homepage. Controls are manual, live near the case title on desktop and follow the story on mobile, and expose equivalent swipe, touch, mouse, and keyboard paths. There is no autoplay.

   Until approved proof exists, three explicitly fictional cases may render only in development or nonproduction review mode. They use no real client name, logo, photo, identity, or public story link and are excluded by the production predicate. Governance stays in the structured data and review layer: `?review=1` adds one quiet `DEMO PROOF` badge outside the case, while the audience-facing composition contains no internal warning copy.

7. **Difference and implementation / deep ink**

   One concise explanation and the ordered sequence Diagnose → Install → Operate → Own. A manual, keyboard-operable stepper exposes one supporting image and explanation at a time while every stage label remains visible. It becomes a vertical sequence on mobile.

8. **Act / sage**

   One still consultation CTA and one quiet book path. No image, moving element, or extra card competes with the decision.

The client-result chapter belongs between How We Help and How It Works. In production it renders only approved records. Until client name, context, intervention, observable shift, result, timeframe, attribution, quote, and display permission are approved, result proof stays out of production audience mode and the Results destination remains gated. Approval of an experience logo does not approve a case claim.

## Motion choreography

- Hero owns one finite atmospheric human-media drift; its headline does not rotate.
- The authority transition owns one slow, continuous logo rail.
- The memory transition follows pain recognition and owns one faster, continuous text rail in the same direction.
- Both rails expose pause/play, pause when focus or fine-pointer hover enters, pause while the tab is hidden, and become static under reduced motion.
- Pain points own manual horizontal movement and flip/reveal disclosure.
- Solution cards use hover/focus preview for optional detail while preserving a real disclosure button and explicit destination link.
- The result chapter uses a manual case-study carousel with no autoplay. Each active case animates its primary number and operating-change graphic once, then holds the final state. Demo records expose no fictional story destination; their CTA leads to the real consultation path.
- The installation stepper changes state only after direct input.
- The final conversion owns stillness.
- Reduced motion renders a complete static page and removes 3D rotation, media drift, kinetic travel, and smooth scrolling.

## Interaction contract

- Carousel arrows move to the previous or next peer card and expose progress.
- Touch and trackpad scrolling use native horizontal scroll snap.
- Card fronts are explicit buttons with `aria-expanded` and a labelled detail region. Fine-pointer hover and keyboard focus preview the same second side; tap/click locks the state.
- Card backs expose a real solution link and a separate Back control.
- No essential meaning or only action is hover-only, and there is no nested interactive wrapper.
- Static imagery does not receive pointer or control styling. Moving rails have real pause/play controls.
- No autoplay carousel or rotating headline.

## Copy and readability

- H1 and H2 stay within four rendered lines at 320px, 390px, tablet, and desktop; two to three are preferred.
- Pain-card fronts contain one label, one recognition headline, and one reveal cue.
- Pain-card labels use quiet sentence case; card backs use natural editorial sentences rather than repeated uppercase mini-headlines.
- Service-card titles appear once. Internal labels may still support state and analytics but do not repeat the visible title.
- With the hero category as the intentional exception, normal homepage sections begin with the headline rather than a redundant eyebrow.
- Solution cards keep one scope, one promise, one short explanation, the engagement family where relevant, and one explicit navigation outcome visible. Secondary change detail may sit behind the labelled disclosure.
- Supporting copy stays under `64ch`.

The homepage hero uses a responsive `720–820px` measure rather than a character-count lock. Reusable `heading-wide`, `heading-medium`, and `heading-narrow` measures cap headings near 900, 780, and 620 pixels before any font-size reduction is considered. The pain-section heading and its supporting sentence form one vertical, bounded composition (approximately `850px` on wide screens). Neither area uses authored `<br>` elements; responsive width and balanced wrapping control the line breaks.

## Conversion and measurement

- Consultation CTAs preserve their source section in the destination URL.
- Pain navigation, pain hover/reveals, problem selections, solution selections, installation-step selections, book clicks, and homepage consultation clicks emit privacy-safe interaction events.
- Event context may include page, section, selection, entry route, and UTM values. It must not include free-text form answers.
- The analytics bridge emits a local `120vc:analytics` event and uses `dataLayer` when an approved analytics adapter is present. Production analytics and consent configuration remain a launch dependency.
- The consultation form uses staged commitment: four business-identification fields first, then one required execution condition plus optional goal and timing.
- The current email-draft review outcome remains an honest development fallback. A verified CRM or form endpoint is required before the site may claim that a request was submitted.

## Proof and publication

- Do not infer approval from a logo appearing on an older website. The current rail is allowed only because the user supplied and explicitly approved `120vc-approved-client-logos.zip` on 2026-08-27.
- Do not publish unsupported performance figures or stock imagery as evidence.
- Results remains proof-gated in the header, footer, metadata, and sitemap behavior.
- Proof availability is derived from separate structured logo and case records with surface-specific permissions and evidence-governance fields. Public discovery also requires the reviewed proof presentation to be wired; records alone cannot open the gate.
- The approved experience-logo rail does not open the Results gate.
- The case-study composition may be tested with structured fictional records in development or review mode, but only approved records may reach the production audience. Demo status is visible in the nonproduction review layer rather than repeated inside the case. The production selector is `proofStatus === "approved" && approvedForHomepage === true`.
- Until at least one production record passes that selector, the internal launch gate remains `PROOF CONTENT REQUIRED BEFORE PRODUCTION CONVERSION LAUNCH`.
